# Visual and copy rules

Use this reference for popup artwork, wording, and responsive presentation.

## Information hierarchy

Aim for one immediate message: restaurant, benefit, action. Remove repeated statements before reducing font size.

- Let the food or restaurant image carry most of the mobile composition.
- State the offer once at headline scale. Do not repeat the same discount in a headline, paragraph, and offer card.
- Ensure the restaurant name is readable independently of its logo, especially in English and German.
- Put restaurant name and short address on separate lines.
- Format location as short street address without postcode plus neighbourhood.
- Keep the primary CTA short and omit decorative arrows unless they convey necessary direction.
- Keep “暂时不要” visually secondary.
- Use an unobtrusive close icon: a white `×` without a visible white circular plate, while preserving an adequate transparent touch target and accessible label.

Do not rigidly reproduce a previous restaurant's layout. Preserve clarity and hierarchy while adapting color, image treatment, and typography to the campaign's material.

## Artwork

Inspect supplied images before editing. Recompose or regenerate when unwanted poster text, QR codes, dates, or decorations are embedded; a crude crop is not a redesign.

- Prefer credible food photography and restaurant-specific details over synthetic abundance that looks AI-generated.
- Preserve enough negative space where coupon-detail cards may overlap hero imagery.
- Do not add claims, dishes, logos, or brand marks unsupported by source material.
- When using restaurant-site images, record the source URL and confirm that reuse is appropriate for the task.
- Keep source and output as separate files when practical; never overwrite the only original.

## Responsive checks

Check actual rendering at desktop and 390px mobile widths. Pay particular attention to image subject visibility, text wrapping, CTA reachability, close-button treatment, and whether restaurant name/address remain separate. Avoid height-only media queries that unintentionally compress short desktop windows; scope compact mobile layout by width.

Verify Chinese, English, and German because word length changes hierarchy and wrapping.
